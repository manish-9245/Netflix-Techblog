# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

Independent authors: solo builders and bloggers who write technical posts and want standout, Netflix-grade engineering writing without a team behind them.

## Product Purpose

Write Like Netflix is an open-source suite of 13 AI skills that teaches assistants to produce Netflix-style engineering blogs. Success means a solo author ships posts with Netflix density: measured claims, real figures, strong hooks, editor-grade structure.

## Positioning

The only writing skill suite trained on the complete Netflix TechBlog archive (800+ posts, 2017 to 2026) with every rule quantified against the corpus. Neighboring prompt packs offer generic advice; this one cites numbers.

## Operating Context

Users work in Claude Code, OpenCode, or other skill-compatible assistants. Entry points: GitHub repo, Claude plugin install, landing page, demo blogs. Evaluation ritual: generate with the skills, score with the built-in reviewer (pass mark 48/70).

## Capabilities and Constraints

13 skills: router, topics, titles, series, research, interview, data story, code, hook, writer, visuals, reviewer, fact-checker. MIT licensed. Landing site is a static static build (HTML, Tailwind CDN, GSAP CDN, nginx on Railway). Blog prose carries no semicolons and no em or en dashes. Proof is restricted to measured corpus numbers, the skill list, and three demo blogs.

## Brand Commitments

Name: Write Like Netflix. Netflix wordmark artwork on site. Voice: direct, quantified, no generic writing advice. Banned: emojis in code and output, cheap meta-labels, invented testimonials or benchmarks.

## Evidence on Hand

Corpus profile of 388 posts (structure, voice, titles, topics, figures, narrative) in local data files. 24 hand-typed figures with house palette. Three demo blogs on LLM serving, workload attestation, and GenRec. Repo: https://github.com/manish-9245/Netflix-Techblog. Live site: https://write-like-netflix-production.up.railway.app.

## Product Principles

1. Numbers over adjectives, in the product and on the page.
2. Every claim traces to something measured.
3. Solo authors get team-grade output, not team-grade process.
4. The suite grades itself: reviewer gate before publish, always.
